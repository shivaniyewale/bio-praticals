Assignment 1 – DNA Sequence Analysis
A. Important theory questions and answers
Q1. Define DNA. Explain its structure and importance.
Answer:

DNA (Deoxyribonucleic Acid) is a genetic material that carries hereditary information in living organisms.

Structure of DNA:
1. DNA has a double-helix structure.
2. It consists of two strands.
3. It contains four nitrogenous bases: Adenine (A), Thymine (T), Guanine (G) and Cytosine (C).
4. A pairs with T, and G pairs with C.
Importance:
- Stores genetic information.
- Transfers hereditary information.
- Controls protein synthesis.
- Helps in growth and development.
Q2. Define DNA Sequence Analysis.
Answer:

DNA Sequence Analysis is the process of studying the order of nucleotides (A, T, G and C) in a DNA sequence to identify genes, motifs, mutations and other important genetic information.
Q3. What is GC content? Explain its importance.
Answer:

GC content is the percentage of Guanine (G) and Cytosine (C) bases present in a DNA sequence.

Formula:
\[
GC\ Content=\frac{G+C}{Total\ Bases}\times100
\]

Importance:
1. Helps study DNA stability.
2. Helps compare DNA sequences.
3. Helps in genome analysis.
4. Provides information about genetic composition.
Example:

DNA sequence: ATGC

G = 1, C = 1, Total = 4.
\[
GC=\frac{1+1}{4}\times100=50\%
\]

Q4. What is a DNA motif? Explain its importance.
Answer:

A DNA motif is a short, specific sequence of nucleotides that has biological importance.

Importance:
- Helps identify functional DNA regions.
- Helps identify regulatory sequences.
- Helps study gene expression.
- Helps understand DNA functions.
Example: ATG is a start codon.
Q5. What are start and stop codons?
Answer:

Codons are groups of three nucleotides that represent amino acids or translation signals.

Start codon:
- ATG is the standard start codon in DNA.
- It signals the beginning of protein translation.
Stop codons:
- TAA
- TAG
- TGA
They signal the end of protein translation.
Q6. What is an ORF?
Answer:

ORF stands for Open Reading Frame. It is a continuous sequence of DNA or RNA that begins with a start codon and ends with a stop codon in the same reading frame.

It helps identify possible protein-coding regions.
Q7. What is a reverse complement sequence?
Answer:

A reverse complement is a DNA sequence obtained by reversing the original sequence and replacing each base with its complementary base.

Base pairing:
- A ↔ T
- G ↔ C
Example:

Original: ATGC

Reverse: CGTA

Reverse complement: GCAT

Importance: It is useful in DNA sequencing, gene analysis and bioinformatics.

Assignment 2 – RNA-Seq Data Analysis
A. Important theory questions and answers
Q1. What is RNA-Seq? Explain its importance in studying gene expression.
Answer:

RNA-Seq (RNA Sequencing) is a technique used to study and measure gene expression by sequencing RNA molecules.

Importance:
1. Identifies expressed genes.
2. Measures gene expression levels.
3. Compares gene expression between samples.
4. Identifies different RNA molecules.
5. Helps in disease research and drug discovery.
Q2. What is gene expression? Explain how RNA-Seq is used to study gene expression.
Answer:

Gene expression is the process by which information stored in a gene is used to produce a functional product, such as RNA or protein.

RNA-Seq workflow:
1. Collect biological samples.
2. Extract RNA.
3. Convert RNA into complementary DNA (cDNA).
4. Perform sequencing.
5. Analyze sequencing data.
6. Measure gene expression levels.
Q3. What is Differential Gene Expression (DGE)? Explain its importance.
Answer:

Differential Gene Expression is the process of identifying differences in gene expression levels between two or more conditions.

Importance:
- Identifies upregulated genes.
- Identifies downregulated genes.
- Helps study diseases.
- Helps understand biological changes.
- Supports drug discovery.
Q4. What is fold change? Explain how it is used to identify upregulated and downregulated genes.
Answer:

Fold Change measures the difference in gene expression between two conditions.

Formula:
\[
Fold\ Change=\frac{Treated\ Expression}{Control\ Expression}
\]

Interpretation in our practical:
- FC ≥ 2: Upregulated.
- FC ≤ 0.5: Downregulated.
- Between 0.5 and 2: No substantial change by the selected threshold.
Example 1:

Control = 50, Treated = 150.
\[
FC=\frac{150}{50}=3
\]

The gene is upregulated.

Example 2:

Control = 200, Treated = 100.
\[
FC=\frac{100}{200}=0.5
\]

The gene is downregulated.

Biological databases are organized collections of biological information such as DNA sequences, RNA sequences, proteins, genes and pathways.

Assignment 3 – Protein Structure Prediction
A. Important theory questions and answers
Q1. What is a protein? Explain its importance in living organisms.
Answer:

A protein is a biological molecule made up of amino acids joined together by peptide bonds.

Importance:
1. Helps in growth and tissue repair.
2. Acts as enzymes to speed up chemical reactions.
3. Transports substances in the body.
4. Helps the immune system fight infections.
5. Supports muscle movement.
6. Provides structure to cells and tissues.
Example: Haemoglobin carries oxygen in the blood.
Q2. Define Protein Sequence Analysis.
Answer:

Protein Sequence Analysis is the process of studying the order of amino acids in a protein to identify its length, composition, molecular weight and possible biological functions.
Q3. What is protein structure prediction? Explain why it is important.
Answer:

Protein Structure Prediction is the process of predicting the three-dimensional (3D) structure of a protein using its amino acid sequence.

Importance:
1. Helps understand protein functions.
2. Helps identify protein interactions.
3. Supports drug discovery.
4. Helps study diseases.
5. Reduces the need for some expensive laboratory experiments.
Q4. What is homology modeling? Explain its basic working.
Answer:

Homology modeling is a technique used to predict the 3D structure of an unknown protein using the known structure of a similar protein as a template.

Working:
1. Enter the target protein sequence.
2. Find a similar protein with a known structure.
3. Align both protein sequences.
4. Build a 3D model using the template.
5. Evaluate the predicted structure.
Q5. What is SWISS-MODEL? Explain how it is used for protein structure prediction.
Answer:

SWISS-MODEL is an online tool used to predict the 3D structure of proteins through homology modeling.

Steps:
1. Open the SWISS-MODEL website.
2. Enter the protein sequence.
3. Search for a suitable template.
4. Select the template.
5. Generate the 3D structure.
6. Analyze the predicted model.
Website: SWISS-MODEL
Q6. Explain the relationship between protein sequence, structure and function.
Answer:

Protein sequence, structure and function are closely related.
1. Sequence: The order of amino acids in a protein.
2. Structure: The 3D shape formed by folding the amino acid sequence.
3. Function: The biological activity performed by the protein.
The amino acid sequence influences protein folding. The resulting structure helps determine its function.

Remember:

Sequence → Structure → Function.

A. Important theory questions and answers
Q1. What is Machine Learning? Explain its applications in bioinformatics.
Answer:

Machine Learning is a branch of Artificial Intelligence that allows computers to learn patterns from data and make predictions without being explicitly programmed for every task.

Applications in bioinformatics:
1. Disease prediction.
2. Gene classification.
3. Protein structure prediction.
4. Drug discovery.
5. DNA sequence analysis.
6. Gene expression analysis.

Genomic data is biological information related to the DNA, genes and genetic characteristics of an organism.

Machine Learning analysis:
1. Collect genomic data.
2. Extract important features.
3. Prepare the dataset.
4. Train the model.
5. Test the model.
6. Predict the class or outcome.

Classification is a supervised Machine Learning technique used to divide data into predefined categories or classes.

In our practical:
1. Collect genomic data.
2. Select GeneFeature1 and GeneFeature2.
3. Assign class labels such as Healthy and Disease.
4. Train the model.
5. Predict the class of new samples.
Q4. What is Random Forest? Explain its basic working and advantages.
Answer:

Random Forest is a supervised Machine Learning algorithm used for classification and regression. It combines multiple decision trees to make a final prediction.

Working:
1. Select random samples from the training dataset.
2. Build multiple decision trees.
3. Each tree makes a prediction.
4. Combine predictions using majority voting for classification.
5. Select the class with the highest votes.
Advantages:
- Good prediction performance in many applications.
- Reduces overfitting compared with a single decision tree in many situations.
- Handles multiple features.
- Can work with large datasets.
- Provides feature importance information.
Q5. Why is genomic data divided into training and testing data? Explain the importance of model accuracy.
Answer:

The dataset is divided into training and testing data to train the model and evaluate its performance on unseen data.

Training data:
- Used to teach the model.
- Helps the model learn patterns.
- In our practical, 70% of the data is used.
Testing data:
- Used to evaluate the model.
- Checks prediction performance on unseen samples.
- In our practical, 30% of the data is used.
Accuracy:

Accuracy measures the proportion of correct predictions.
\[
Accuracy=\frac{Correct\ Predictions}{Total\ Predictions}\times100
\]

Example: If 9 out of 10 predictions are correct, accuracy = 90%.
